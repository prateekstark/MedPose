# MedPose novelty check

Checked **2026-11-05**; coverage extends into November 2026 only through this date. This is a bounded, partially access-limited search, not proof of absence. **All three claims: PARTLY DONE.** The exact combined experiment was NOT FOUND in the sources inspected. That does not establish priority. Proposed remaining contributions below are research recommendations, not established novelty claims.

## 1. One fitting method, one through four views, marker-based angle validation

| Closest primary paper | Year / venue | Population and method | Relation to proposed claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| [OpenCap: Human movement dynamics from smartphone videos](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011462) | 2023, PLOS Computational Biology | Healthy adults; video pose detection, triangulation, anatomical marker augmentation and biomechanical analysis versus laboratory marker-based measurements. | VERIFIED: Methods/Validation sensitivity analysis compares **two, three and five cameras**, including alternative detectors. Camera-count accuracy is already studied. Does not supply the requested single-view-to-four-view sweep. The [author reproduction script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencap-org/opencap-core/main/ReproducePaperResults/labValidationVideosToKinematics.py) makes these configurations explicit. |
| [Impact of Multi-View Fusion and Biomechanical Modeling on Markerless Motion Tracking](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41091616/) | 2025 online / 2026 issue, IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering | Healthy participants; monocular estimators and biomechanical variants compared with multiview systems against marker-based motion capture. | VERIFIED: directly challenges novelty of comparing monocular, multiview and biomechanical constraints. Different systems/view counts are compared; a fixed fitting method evaluated at every count from one to four was NOT FOUND in the inspected abstract. Full experimental coverage remains a reading gap. |
| [Influence of camera geometry on 3D joint angle estimation in markerless motion capture](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1850018/full) | 2026, Frontiers in Sports and Active Living | Female soccer athletes; Pose2Sim-based processing of two-camera combinations, evaluated against Theia3D multiview estimates. | VERIFIED: camera geometry, anatomical plane, task and dropout are explicit outcomes (Abstract/Methods). **Markerless reference**, not marker-based ground truth; fixed two-view combinations, not a one-to-four sweep. Geometry must be controlled in a camera-count experiment. |
| [OpenCap Monocular](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24733v1) | 2026, arXiv preprint | Healthy adults performing walking, squats and sit-to-stand; monocular reconstruction and biomechanical fitting evaluated against marker-based measurements. | VERIFIED: §§2.4–3.1 establish a single-view biomechanical validation and comparison with multiview OpenCap. Different pipelines do not isolate camera count within one method. |

**Verdict: PARTLY DONE.** Broad claims of the first camera-count study or the first monocular-versus-multiview biomechanical validation are contradicted by the papers above. The exact conjunction—unchanged fitting objective, all counts one/two/three/four, per-joint and per-plane angle errors, marker-based reference—was NOT FOUND in the inspected material.

**Candidate remaining contribution:** a preregistered paired ablation using the same people, trials, reference model, calibration, synchronization, fitting objective and hyperparameter-selection procedure. Separate view count from view geometry using multiple camera subsets. Report raw and corrected angle errors, failures and coverage, with subject-level uncertainty; make the monocular assumptions explicit. Adding a view must not silently change the method or evaluation cohort. Clinical CP validation would strengthen the contribution but requires a suitable reference dataset.

## 2. Assistive devices, helpers and controlled occlusion

| Closest primary paper | Year / venue | Population and method | Relation to proposed claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Portable Biomechanics Lab journal version](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02825-w) | 2026, npj Digital Medicine | Controls, neurological conditions, lower-limb prosthesis users and pediatric participants; monocular biomechanical fitting against clinical multiview markerless estimates. | VERIFIED: Results/Fig.2 and Methods include assistive devices and therapist assistance. “Works with a walker/helper” alone is already demonstrated. This does not establish a controlled device-occlusion penalty against marker-based truth. |
| [Calibrated Uncertainty for Trustworthy Clinical Gait Analysis Using Probabilistic Multiview Markerless Motion Capture](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22412v2) | 2026, arXiv version inspected | Clinical multiview fitting with uncertainty; pediatric Shriners cohort includes CP and crutch/posterior-walker use, with Vicon reference. | VERIFIED: §§2.2–2.4 include the target clinical/device setting and marker-based comparison. A device-stratified causal occlusion experiment or matched synthetic-video intervention was NOT FOUND. Particularly close prior work; discuss explicitly. |
| [Multi-view Pose Fusion for Occlusion-Aware 3D Human Pose Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15810) | 2024, ECCV workshops | Human3.6M-derived occluded benchmark and robot workcell data; fuse monocular 3D poses with reprojection and skeletal constraints. | VERIFIED: synthetic occlusion plus multiview fusion is already studied. Endpoints are 3D pose rather than clinical joint-angle validity in children with walking aids. |
| [How Robust is 3D Human Pose Estimation to Occlusion?](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09316) | 2018, IROS Robotic Co-workers 4.0 workshop | Human3.6M pose-estimation experiments with synthetic occlusion and augmentation. | VERIFIED: adding occlusion to ground-truth-labelled imagery is an established evaluation strategy. Clinical device geometry, helper identity confusion and biomechanical angle fidelity are different endpoints. |

**Verdict: PARTLY DONE.** Both clinical assistive-device use and synthetic-occlusion robustness have prior art. A first-ever walker-capable or synthetic-occlusion claim is untenable on this evidence. The narrower controlled combination was NOT FOUND in the inspected papers, not proven absent.

**Candidate remaining contribution:** evaluate real CP/device/helper videos and paired controlled occlusion on reference-labelled videos. Separate device type, visible-body fraction, occlusion duration, view count, self-occlusion and person-identity failures. Preserve the underlying reference and explicitly distinguish image masking from physically changing gait with a device. Measure joint/plane error and tracking failure, not only successful-frame error. Validate synthetic effects against real device cases; synthetic overlays alone cannot establish clinical robustness.

## 3. Preservation of involuntary movement and frequency response

| Closest primary paper | Year / venue | Population and method | Relation to proposed claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Validation and application of computer vision algorithms for video-based tremor analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01153-1) | 2024, npj Digital Medicine | Essential-tremor/DBS context; computer-vision hand tracking, including MediaPipe, compared with motion capture and accelerometry. | VERIFIED from publisher-indexed text: tremor amplitude and dominant frequency are evaluated. Tremor preservation is not an untouched topic. Direct publisher navigation failed; indexed primary text was available. Does not establish full-body biomechanical fitting gain/phase over a controlled frequency sweep. |
| [Accuracy of Smartphone Video for Contactless Measurement of Hand Tremor Frequency](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8607978/) | 2021, Movement Disorders Clinical Practice | Parkinsonian, essential and functional tremor; smartphone-video optical flow and spectral analysis versus accelerometry. | VERIFIED: contactless frequency measurement is already validated. This is not a 3D musculoskeletal reconstruction or joint-angle transfer-function study. |
| [Towards automated video-based assessment of dystonia in dyskinetic cerebral palsy](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2023.1108114/full) | 2023, Frontiers in Robotics and AI | Children and young adults with dyskinetic CP; resting sitting/lying videos, DeepLabCut 2D tracking, movement/position features and random-forest regression to dystonia ratings. | VERIFIED: §§2.1–2.4 directly address the target disorder. Dystonia assessment from pose is already done. Manual landmark and clinical-rating comparisons do not establish preservation of 3D joint-angle amplitude, phase or irregular motion. |
| [Simultaneous hyperkinetic movement disorders phenotyping: a cross-cohort pediatric transfer study using routine videos, markerless pose estimation and a tabular foundation model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07674) | 2026, arXiv; record cites Frontiers in Neurology publication | Adult development and pediatric monogenic movement-disorder transfer cohorts; pose-derived kinematic descriptors and classification. | VERIFIED: extends prior art beyond isolated tremor to hyperkinetic phenotyping and pediatric transfer. Classification success is not a measurement of biomechanical signal fidelity or smoothing attenuation. |

**Verdict: PARTLY DONE.** Video measurement of tremor frequency/amplitude and pose-based dystonia assessment are established. A general claim that existing video pipelines cannot preserve involuntary movement would be contradicted by the first two papers. The specific full-body biomechanical frequency-response experiment was NOT FOUND in this search.

**Candidate remaining contribution:** quantify amplitude gain, phase delay, coherence and waveform error against an appropriate synchronized reference, across frequency and amplitude, for the complete fitting pipeline. Ablate temporal priors and filters. Include irregular recorded movements as well as controlled periodic signals; do not reduce every movement disorder to a tremor frequency. Compare stages before and after fitting to localize attenuation. Report frame rate, exposure, reference filtering, dropped frames and uncertainty; do not call an injected angle trajectory an image-level benchmark unless it is rendered into the video input.

A concrete baseline to investigate is OpenCap Monocular: **Supplementary Table S1 specifies filtering at 6 Hz for walking, 4 Hz for squat/sit-to-stand, and 8 Hz for other activities**, with a smoothness term. Those parameters motivate measurement; they are not themselves an empirical frequency-response result. [Primary supplement](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24733v1)
